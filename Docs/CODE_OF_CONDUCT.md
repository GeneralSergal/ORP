# ORP — Code of Conduct
## ORP v3.0 — Governance‑Aligned Participation Rules

This document defines the behavioral and epistemic standards for participation in the ORP ecosystem.  
It aligns with the ORP v3.0 governance model, emphasizing signal integrity, provenance preservation, and strict layer isolation.

---

## 1. Core Principle

Participation in ORP must prioritize:

- signal integrity over narrative fluency
- provenance preservation over stylistic preference
- explicit uncertainty over implicit speculation
- recoverable reasoning over coherent fabrication
- structural correctness over rhetorical appeal

ORP is a governance‑first epistemic system.  
All contributors must respect its invariants.

---

## 2. Expected Standards

Participants must:

- maintain strict separation between evidence, interpretation, and inference
- preserve epistemic boundaries across L1–L4
- expose uncertainty explicitly
- avoid unsupported causal claims
- maintain structural consistency with ORP architecture
- respect fail‑closed reasoning behavior
- preserve provenance in all transformations
- align contributions with ORP_RUNTIME.md and ORP_SYSTEM_MAP.md

Constructive adversarial testing is encouraged when grounded in evidence and structural reasoning.

---

## 3. Prohibited Behavior

### 3.1 Epistemic Violations
- presenting unverified claims as factual
- collapsing epistemic categories (e.g., inference → evidence)
- laundering speculation into verified form
- masking uncertainty through narrative coherence
- introducing ambiguity that obscures provenance

### 3.2 Structural Violations
- bypassing runtime or schema constraints
- merging responsibilities across ORP layers
- introducing undocumented behavioral changes
- violating L1–L4 isolation rules
- modifying governance logic without alignment

### 3.3 Community Violations
- harassment, personal attacks, or intimidation
- bad‑faith argumentation or disruption
- intentionally misleading benchmark submissions
- fabricated evaluation results or provenance
- attempts to obscure drift, decay, or failure signals

---

## 4. Enforcement Model

ORP uses a structural enforcement model rather than subjective moderation.

Violations may result in:

- rejection of contributions
- invalidation of evaluation artifacts
- rollback of incompatible architectural changes
- removal of misleading or corrupted material
- requirement to re‑establish provenance

Enforcement decisions prioritize:

- structural correctness
- epistemic integrity
- drift visibility
- governance alignment

---

## 5. Scope

This code of conduct applies to:

- repository discussions
- pull requests
- benchmark submissions
- evaluation artifacts
- architectural proposals
- documentation contributions
- issue reports

It complements, and does not override:

- ORP_RUNTIME.md
- ORP_EVALUATION_SCHEMA.md
- ORP_SYSTEM_MAP.md
- repository licensing terms

---

## 6. Design Principle

Visible uncertainty is preferable to invisible corruption.  
Signal integrity is the primary system value.

---

## 7. Final Statement

Participation in ORP requires adherence to governance‑aligned epistemic discipline.  
All contributors share responsibility for maintaining isolation integrity, drift observability, and provenance preservation.
