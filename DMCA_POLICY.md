# **DMCA_POLICY.md**  
### **Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) Takedown Policy**  
*(Applies to all public deployments of this software)*

---

## **1. Purpose of This Policy**

This document describes the procedure for submitting copyright infringement notices (DMCA takedown requests) related to **content uploaded by users** to any public instance of this software.

This policy applies **only** to hosted deployments.  
It does **not** apply to the open‑source codebase itself.

---

## **2. Nature of the Software**

This project is an **open‑source, self‑hosted application**.  
The project maintainers:

- do **not** operate a centralized service  
- do **not** receive or store user content  
- do **not** have access to data uploaded to third‑party deployments  

If you are using a public instance operated by someone else, your DMCA request must be directed to **that operator**, not the project maintainers.

## **4. Submitting a Valid DMCA Takedown Notice**

To file a valid DMCA notice, your request **must** include all of the following:

1. Identification of the copyrighted work you claim has been infringed.  
2. Identification of the specific content you believe is infringing (URL or file path).  
3. Your full legal name and contact information.  
4. A statement that you have a good‑faith belief the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.  
5. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act on their behalf.  
6. Your physical or electronic signature.

Incomplete notices may be rejected.

---

## **5. What Happens After a Valid Notice Is Received**

If a valid DMCA notice is received for a deployment operated by the maintainers:

- The allegedly infringing content may be removed or disabled.  
- The user who uploaded the content may be notified (if contact information is available).  
- A record of the request may be retained for legal compliance.  

If a valid **counter‑notification** is received, the maintainers will:

- **promptly forward** the counter‑notification to the original complainant  
- **generally restore** the removed material within **10 to 14 business days**,  
  **unless** the original complainant notifies us that they have filed a court action seeking to restrain the user from engaging in the infringing activity  

This process is required under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g).

For third‑party deployments, the operator is responsible for handling takedown requests.

---

## **6. Counter‑Notification Procedure**

If you believe content was removed in error, you may submit a counter‑notification including:

1. Identification of the removed content and its location before removal.  
2. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe the removal was a mistake or misidentification.  
3. Your name, address, and phone number.  
4. **A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district in which your address is located (or the federal courts in the jurisdiction of the website operator if outside the U.S.), and that you will accept service of process from the person who provided the original infringement notification.**  
5. Your physical or electronic signature.

Upon receiving a valid counter‑notification, the maintainers will follow the legally required forwarding and restoration process described in Section 5.

---

## **7. No Duty to Monitor**

The maintainers and operators:

- do **not** proactively monitor user content  
- do **not** review uploads for copyright compliance  
- do **not** guarantee detection of infringing material  

However, they may take action when legally required.

This aligns with the safe‑harbor provisions of the DMCA.

---

## **8. Repeat Infringer Policy**

Operators of public instances may, at their discretion:

- suspend  
- restrict  
- or permanently block  

users who repeatedly upload infringing content.

This is not an obligation but a permitted protective measure.

---

## **9. Third‑Party Deployments**

If you are using a deployment **not operated by the maintainers**, you must contact the operator of that instance directly.

The project maintainers:

- cannot remove content from third‑party servers  
- cannot forward DMCA notices to third‑party operators  
- cannot act as intermediaries  

---

## **10. Amendments**

This policy may be updated at any time to comply with:

- changes in law  
- hosting provider requirements  
- operational needs  

Operators of public instances may adapt this policy for their own deployments.
